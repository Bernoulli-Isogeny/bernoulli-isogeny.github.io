  • 10:00 - 11:00: Pierrick Dartois: The module isogeny key exchange (MIKE): a fast and compact post-quantum NIKE with 4-dimensional isogenies

    Abstract MIKE is a post-quantum non-interactive key exhange (NIKE), built as a Diffie-Hellman key exchange based on the module action on oriented abelian varieties. Under the hood, this scheme involves a key generation phase in dimension two, and a shared key derivation phase in dimension four. Despite the complex mathematical objects involved, MIKE is actually very fast in practice for an isogeny based scheme and can run below 5 ms on a laptop at NIST-I security level, while achieving record breaking compacity (with 80 B public keys at NIST-I level).
    This great performance follows from specific properties of the module action that allow algorithmic gains. In this talk, we shall see that the key generation can be accelarated by exploiting Scholten's construction and that the shared key derivation phase greatly benefits from the symmetries of the abelian fourfolds that we encounter.

  • 11:00 - 12:00: Giacomo Pope: Engineering MIKE: an implementation first perspective

    Abstract MIKE is a newly proposed post-quantum, Isogeny-based NIKE which requires a meager 100 pages of appendices to describe. In this talk, we’ll go through MIKE key generation and shared secrets from an engineering perspective, walking through the building blocks to give a concrete description of what is required. We will also mention future work in creating optimised and cryptographically safe implementations for various hardware which can feed possible projects in the immediate future

  • 12:00 - 13:30: Lunch break

  • 13:30 - 14:15: Wouter Castryck: Shimura class group actions on oriented superspecial PPAVs

  • 14:15 - 15:00: Sabrina Kunzweiler: Computing n-isogenies in level-n

    Abstract With the aim of finding efficient formulas for arithmetic and isogeny computations, a variety of different models of elliptic curves has been analyzed in the past. Less is known about computationally efficient models for principally polarized abelian varieties in higher dimensions. This talk is about models induced by symmetric theta structures. We present recent advances concerning their computational aspects, with a focus on level-2 and level-3 models.

Tuesday

  • 09:00 - 10:10: Olivier Taïbi: Rapid mixing in superspecial isogeny graphs

    Abstract I will explain joint work with Maria Corte-Real Santos, Harun Kir and Benjamin Wesolowski in which we prove expansion properties of isogeny graphs of superspecial abelian varieties, including with level structures.

  • 10:10 - 10:30: Coffee break

  • 10:30 - 11:15: Benjamin Smith : Binary isogeny-based cryptography

    Abstract What happens if we do isogeny-based cryptography over fields of characteristic 2? This talk will survey several results - some constructive, some destructive - with many co-authors and collaborators.

  • 11:15 - 12:00: Hiroshi Onuki : Delta-KLPT+: Improved Norm Bounds for the Quaternion Isogeny Path Problem

    Abstract This talk introduces a new KLPT-type algorithm, called Delta-KLPT+, that improves the norm bound from p^{4.5} in GeneralizedKLPT to p^4. This is joint work with Kohei Nakagawa.

  • 12:00 - 13:30: Lunch break

  • 13:30 - 14:15: Giulio Codogni: Spectral theory, mixing time and enumeration of cycles for isogenies graphs

    Abstract I will review some properties and open questions about isogeny graphs. I will first focus on elliptic curves, and then consider abelian varieties. I will discuss properites of the spectrum of adjacency matrices of these graphs, the mixing time, the total number of cycles, and whether these cycles cluster in some special regions. The talk is based on a joint work and ongiong discussions with Guido Lido.

  • 14:15 - 15:00: Sarah Arpin: Isogeny graphs of abelian varieties and singular ideals in orders

    Abstract This is joint work with Stefano Marseglia and Caleb Springer. In this work, we provide a structure theorem for the connected components of isogeny graphs of unpolarized abelian varieties of arbitrary dimension with commutative endomorphism algebra, whose endomorphism rings contain a fixed locally Bass order.

  • 15:00 - 15:30: Coffee break

  • 15:30 - 16:15: Annamaria Iezzi: Generating sets for supersingular endomorphism rings

  • 16:15 - 17:00: Eda Kirimli: Detecting Split Surfaces and Real Multiplication on Superspecial Abelian Surfaces

    Abstract We study principally polarized superspecial abelian surfaces over finite fields using their refined Humbert invariants. We show how primitive representations by these invariants characterize the existence of ( N , N ) -isogenies from Jacobians to products of elliptic curves. This leads to an understanding of splitting levels and shortest paths to the product locus in the associated isogeny graphs. We also explore the connection between primitive representations and embeddings of real quadratic orders into endomorphism rings, providing insight into the existence of real multiplication. This is joint work with Gaurish Korpal.

  • 11:15 - 12:00: Elisa Lorenzo Garcia: Refined Humbert invariants and subvarieties of A_2(C): the rank 3 case

    Abstract We study refined Humbert invariants of rank (3), focusing on the case of discriminant (-16d), where (-d) is primitive. In particular, we show that, for such a given refined Humbert invariant, there is a single Galois orbit. I will conclude with some perspectives on potential applications to the navigation of isogeny graphs

    Abstract The rise of MIKE has many wondering (again) if there is, perhaps, a way of applying Kuperberg to the computational supersingular isogeny problem. In this talk I will discuss some (as yet) failed approaches that I have tried, some old (using class group actions of Weil restrictions), and some new (using MIKE, and using refined Humbert invariants).
